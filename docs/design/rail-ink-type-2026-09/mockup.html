Rail & Ink Round

Nine asks from George in one contract: menus as text, shortcuts that nest, a one-line thread list with honest glyphs, ink that is grey rather than white, a type stack with more character, a question card that stops drawing a box inside a box, a head that is just the mark and the fold, and hover that gives a one-line row its facts back. Every stage below is built from the app's real classes and tokens; the "Now" columns are locked to today's values, the "Proposed" columns obey the bar.

Code as its own mode, not another shortcut

A code session is a different animal from a thread: it belongs to a repo and a branch, not a project and a room; its states are streaming / awaiting approval / completed rather than the board's FSM; and its surfaces are the diff, the terminal and the approval card. Mixing them into the thread list is what produced rows like Code · e2e-local · main. Gemini's Chat | Spark is the right shape: one segmented switch under the head, and the rail changes what it lists, never its anatomy. This is the shell's single mode switch, not a destination growing a tab strip (docs/33 §8), and it is the only place the rail changes shape. It depends on the engineering-os work (the Codex branch) landing; the rail below is drawn to that branch's session model.

Hover: the row tells the rest

A one-line row can afford to drop facts because hover gives them back. A thread row shows a card with the full title, the first line of its snippet, and everything the row no longer carries: project, room, branch, age. At the trailing edge the row's fact swaps for two controls, rename and the ⋯ menu (rename · archive · copy link · delete), which is where today's hidden archive button already lives. A project row, in the folder variant and in the picker, gets Codex's pair on hover: new chat here and the ⋯ menu, and a card with what the project is: its counts, its rooms, its repo, and Edit project. Cards are the elevated stratum: --card, one hairline, the pop shadow, never a native title.

Row glyphs say what kind of thing a row is; the pulse says it wants you

Today every task row leads with the same 6px dot, and the dot pulses for liveness and for needs-you alike, so a settled thread and a live one look like siblings. The proposed vocabulary gives each session kind an outline glyph in --dim, keeps colour for state only while a task is active, and spends animation on exactly two things: a run in progress and a question waiting on you.

4

Ink: one step down from white, and chrome wears the body grey

Two changes, and the second matters more. The darks' --text drops from #eaeaea to #cbcbcb (George asked for more than the first step; the bar still carries step 1 at #d9d9d9 to compare), 10.8:1 on the panel. And the rail, the composer hints and message bodies stop reaching for --text at all: rows rest in --body, titles in --text only when selected, meta in --dim. That is what makes Claude read as grey — its primary ink is #faf9f5, near white, but almost nothing in its chrome uses it. The papers keep their ink and lift --dim into AA-large, since today it fails on both.

5

Type: what Claude and Ghostty use, and what we can

Claude sets its interface in Anthropic Sans, with Anthropic Serif for headlines and Anthropic Mono for code, a family drawn for the 2025 brand and not licensable; before that it ran Styrene B with Tiempos. Ghostty embeds JetBrains Mono as its default terminal face, and its window chrome is the OS font. NeuraMesh already runs on Geist for UI text, Geist Mono for kickers, Source Serif 4 for display, all bundled and offline. So the family is not what reads as basic. The treatment is: near-white ink, rail titles at weight 700, 9px kickers in the same mono as the terminal. The specimen below sets the same rail and paragraph in Geist with the new treatment and in three free faces that share Anthropic Sans's humanist warmth. Switch the sans in the bar to try each across the whole page. Each cell carries a badge that says whether its face really loaded; a red fallback means the page is offline and that cell is showing the system font. The 23px line is the tell: the R leg, the double-storey g, the 1 and 7, and the @ differ between all four.

Recommendation. Retreat the type before replacing it: grey ink, rail titles at 450–560 instead of 700, body 13.5px at 1.55, kickers kept but demoted to --dim. A question is a section of the message, not a card in a card

Today an agent bubble is a hairline on --panel — the same colour as the thread behind it — and the question card inside it is a second hairline on --card, five points lighter. Two edges, no material, so the eye reads a box in a box. The unit card already had this ruled on in August: "an embossed card read as a foreign panel inside a conversation; now it is a line of the transcript." The same rule, applied to every answerable card, gives two shapes.

The frame: the conversation owns the sheet, the Workbench is its card, the tabs get a dock of their own

Today a file, an article or a whiteboard opened from a thread replaces the conversation in the main area — which is why "Back to chat ⌘1" exists at all — and the Workbench sits as a third column on the frame. George's read (2026-09-04, Codex as the reference): the conversation should own the sheet edge to edge; the Workbench is the open thread's own details, so it belongs inside the thread as a card floating on the sheet; and the tab strip — with its + menu and every content kind it opens — moves into a side dock at the right edge, toggled from the frame top's side-panel icon. Content opened from a thread lands there as a tab beside the conversation instead of over it. Three columns: rail · sheet (conversation + Workbench card) · dock (tabs). The dock is resizable and collapsible; the Workbench toggle moves into the thread header with a glyph of its own.
