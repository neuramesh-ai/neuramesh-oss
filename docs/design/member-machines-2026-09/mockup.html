Per-member cloud machines — the surfaces

design round · 2026-09-03 · Individual: your one machine. Team: a machine for every member.

00Two plans, never confused

Individual is one person: one cloud machine (the runner), your subscriptions signed in on it, one seat. Team is where a second human begins — and where every member gets a machine of their own. The ids stay free / cloud; the words change on every surface, and every upgrade screen names the plan you are on and the one you are offered. The invitation is the door: on Individual it is refused with Team named, on Team the first one quietly makes the runner yours and mints a fresh one.

00aThe rule on Team — two kinds of machine, one grant

A workspace has one runner (BYOK keys at spawn, the starter brain, no logins — everyone may shell into it) and one machine per member (their vendor logins on its volume — only they may shell into it). A member machine is born asleep: a row and a StatefulSet at zero replicas, no pod, no disk, $0. It wakes when its owner needs it, when a message from its owner arrives, or when the routing ladder needs a subscription only it holds. Sharing is the existing grant (0119): shared with the whole workspace by default, one switch to stop.

The ladder gains one rung, at the bottom
  1. incapable → never. unchanged
  2. designated (continuity · your pick · your default) unchanged
  3. origin — my owner asked → claim unchanged
  4. consent — not lent to the asker → stay out unchanged
  5. failover — no origin machine can serve → claim unchanged
  6. wake a sleeper — nobody awake can serve, but a lent cloud machine that published the runtime is asleep → ask the fleet to wake it, wait inside the boot grace, then let it claim. new — this is what makes a 3 a.m. routine on a Claude subscription run at all

02Sharing — one master switch above the per-member ones

The grant already exists per member (compute.shares, '*' = everyone, on since 0119). What George asked for is the workspace-level answer: is my compute shared with this workspace, yes or no. The master switch writes ['*'] or [] through the same member.share_compute path; the per-member switches below it keep the fine grain and show the intent, never a recomputed set.
