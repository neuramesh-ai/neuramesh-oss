Two states, because only two are distinguishable

What got cut, and why

An earlier draft named five stages — finding hardware, attaching the disk, pulling the image. They were real, measured from an actual cold start, and they were the wrong thing to show: nobody waiting on a terminal wants a tour of the storage driver.

Naming them was also a promise we could not keep. The app can see that a machine was asked for and that it came online; the middle is opaque without new reporting from the fleet. Plain language removed a backend dependency rather than just softening the copy.

Showing one state at a time then cut one more. “Starting” was the instant desired_replicas → 1 write — under a second, so as a solo state it flashed and vanished, and next to “Initializing” it drew a distinction that is ours, not the reader’s. What is left is what a person can actually tell apart: the machine is coming up, then we are attaching to it.

The cost of one state: the first one lasts about two minutes. With the ledger gone, the only moving things are the orb and the elapsed counter — so a slow boot can read as a stuck one. That is the trade for calm, and it is the reason the elapsed time is on screen at all rather than tucked away. If it feels frozen in practice, the answer is a quieter second line after ~45s, not a fake bar.

Variant B · for comparison

Full screen, and why it is the second choice

Press Show as full screen above to see it. It is more cinematic, and it costs more than it looks: it blocks the whole app for two minutes over a request that was scoped to one pane. Someone who opens a terminal while reading a thread loses the thread.

Full screen earns its place in one case — first-run, where there is nothing else to do yet and the boot is the product’s first impression. Elsewhere, pane-scoped keeps the moment without taking the room.

What this does not solve

The wait itself

Every pixel here is managing a 146-second cold start. Warm capacity takes that to roughly 15 seconds, at which point this screen is a flicker rather than an experience — and the anticipatory-wake question stops being a cost calculation. The best version of this design is one nobody looks at for long.
