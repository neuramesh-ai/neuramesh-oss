## Problem Statement

George, 2026-09-07, on the round-1 Foundry mockups: "neuramesh is no longer just a developer tool
as the current screens make it look with the structured developer workflows of plan review, etc."

Round 1 argued the product through the engineering loop: a task thread with a plan card, a pull
request, CI, and an Accept gate. Every section after the hero was a developer section. The
product today runs four kinds of rooms (general, build, marketing, research), a marketing HQ with
scored playbooks, drafted posts and articles with images, whiteboards, routines on a calendar,
files, and skills. None of that was on the page, and the hero frame was a hand-built dev thread.

Two asks in one: reframe the copy from the hero to the footer, and replace the hand-built frame
with a real capture of the current UI.

## Demand Evidence

Unchanged from the 2026-08-28 doc, not re-asked: signups exist, no outside human has been watched
reaching a first agent reply. Nothing on the page may claim proof. No logos, no testimonials, no
usage numbers.

## Status Quo

The enemy stays one shape: a single agent, in a single session, on a single machine, with no second
human and nothing that outlives the run. For the non-developer half of a founder's week the shape
is the same with different tabs: ChatGPT for posts, a scheduler for publishing, a doc for the brand
voice, and no trail of who approved what. Two memories, two approval stories, one person holding
both together.

## Target User and Narrowest Wedge

Unchanged: the solo founder or individual builder who already pays for Claude, OpenAI, or Gemini.
The wedge stays a first agent reply in the browser, in one session, with no install and no key.
What widens is the door: the first reply can be a marketing plan, a research report, or a sketch,
not only code. The page has to say so above the fold.

## Landscape (searched 2026-09-07, generalized terms only)

- Layer 1, what everyone knows: the category sells "AI teammates" and "AI employees" that live
  inside the tools people already use. Notion turned its workspace into a hub for custom agents
  (May 2026). ClickUp ships Super Agents. Lindy sells AI teammates in Slack and Teams. Viktor's line
  is "Not a tool. A hire."
- Layer 2, the discourse: "agentic workspace" is the phrase of the year (Taskade), and solopreneur
  stacks bundle research, writing, and scheduling into one marketing workspace (Brand Brain).
  Founders wire multi-agent companies together by hand (Paperclip plus Claude Code).
- Layer 3, where the convention is wrong for us: everyone sells autonomy and nobody sells the
  approval. No product in that set pairs engineering, marketing, and research under one named crew,
  with rooms the server enforces, on machines the user owns. So the page does not say "AI
  teammates". It says rooms, a crew, and your approval, and it shows the breadth through the rooms.

Sources: vellum.ai (workspace agents compared), lava.so (six agent workspaces compared, Aug 2026),
techcrunch.com (Notion custom agents, 2026-05-13), taskade.com (agentic workspaces), brandbrain.app
(solopreneur marketing agent), mindstudio.ai (Paperclip and Claude Code).

## Constraints

- The hero triad is locked: Your people. Your agents. Your projects.
- The primary CTA stays "Start free in your browser". Facts: 500 free credits, no install, no key,
  no card. Pricing stays Individual (free) and Team ($22 per seat per month).
- ASD-STE100 for every string. No em dashes. No semicolons. Active voice. Simple tenses.
- Both site themes, graphite and light paper, verified.
- The product frame is a real capture of the current UI from the preview harness
  (`apps/desktop/src/renderer/preview`), never an invented layout.
- No invented proof. The proof strip stays bring-your-own-subscriptions.

## Premises

1. The page must show the breadth of the product (rooms, marketing, research, routines,
   whiteboards) without hiding the engineering loop, which is still the strongest proof of the
   approval model. Agreed by the ask itself.
2. The room is the right unit to show breadth, because it is the product's real spine
   (Workspace, Projects, Channels, docs/06) and because it is where an agent's access is enforced.
3. The loop generalizes without changing its mechanics: a plan you review, work in the open,
   a check by someone who did not do the work, and a last step only a human can take. It holds
   for a pull request, a campaign, and a post.
4. "Crew" is the word for the agents on every surface of the page. "Team" is the paid plan.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Rooms first (recommended)
Section 01 is the four rooms, each with the work it runs and what the human gate is there.
The loop follows, generalized. The crew gains the marketer. A short "in every room" strip covers
whiteboards, routines, files, and brains. Rules, surfaces, pricing, and the close stay.
Effort: S (copy and one capture). Risk: low. Reuses the round-1 system and template.
Con: the page grows by one section.

### Approach B: Marketing-first door
The 2026-08-28 hero lead ("give your team one thing: your website") returns, and engineering moves
to the second half. Effort: S. Risk: medium. It over-corrects: a page that leads with marketing
reads as a marketing tool to the developer who is still the best-fit first user.

### Approach C: The loop only, generalized
Keep round 1's spine and only rewrite the loop cells to be function-neutral. Effort: XS.
Risk: high. The screens still show a dev thread, and the breadth claim stays a sentence.

## Recommended Approach

A. Rooms first, with the generalized loop as the trust argument, the marketing content task
(#1025) as the hero capture, and the engineering task (#1046) as the loop's evidence.

Proof strip
- Kicker: Bring your own subscriptions
- Marks: Claude · OpenAI · Gemini
- Line: Sign in to Claude, OpenAI, or Gemini on your machine. Your keys never leave it. neuramesh
  never marks up tokens.

01 The rooms
- Title: #build ships. #marketing tells the world. Every room runs one kind of work.
- Lead: A room holds the people, the agents, the files, and the rules for one kind of work.
  An agent sees a room only when you register it there.
- # build. Plans, pull requests, reviews, and releases. Code reaches main only through your
  accept. (Engineering)
- # marketing. Brand docs, posts, articles, and images on a calendar. Every draft holds until you
  approve it. (Growth)
- # research. Audits, teardowns, and benchmarks that end in a scored report. It says what it could
  not determine. (Research)
- # general. The room where you ask. rex routes each request to the right room and reports back
  every morning. (The front door)

02 The loop
- Title: One agent gives you output. A crew gives you something to approve.
- Lead: Every unit of work starts with a plan you review. Someone who did not do the work checks
  it. The last step is yours. This holds for a pull request, a campaign, and a post.
- 01 Ask. Type it in any room. rex writes the plan and names who does what. Nothing starts until
  you approve. (Human gate)
- 02 Work. The crew works in the open. Every step, file, and screenshot lands in the thread.
  (Evidence required)
- 03 Check. A different agent checks the result against your definition of done. No agent checks
  its own work. (Server enforced)
- 04 Approve. Accept the pull request. Schedule the post. Approve the plan. Only a human can press
  these. (Human only)

03 The crew
- Title: The same named crew, in every room.
- Lead: Most tools spawn a stranger per task and destroy it. neuramesh keeps one crew on your work.
  Review corrections become lessons they keep. Week four beats week one.
- Roster: rex, orchestrator, morning digest for #build · atlas, architect, writes plan v2 for #1046
  · iris, designer, mockup round 2 for the pricing page · patch, developer, works #1046, PR #368
  open · scout, reviewer, reviews #1043 · plume, marketer, two X posts hold for your approval ·
  bosun, shipper, holds the ship gate · Hire a specialist. It joins over A2A.
- Note: "spawn a stranger per task" and "Week four beats week one" are mechanism claims, not
  proof claims. Both ran on the live site's Why named agents band, approved 2026-07.

In every room (strip)
- Whiteboards. Ask for a sketch. A board lands in the thread and opens as a canvas you can edit.
- Routines. Say it once. It runs on a schedule and reports in the room.
- Files. Brand docs, plans, and reports, one shelf per project. Agents read what you allow.
- Brains. Pick a pack, or set one model per role. Bring your own subscriptions.

04 The rules
- Title: Rules the server enforces. Not rules a prompt suggests.
- Lead: Every room carries a policy. An agent registered to #marketing never sees #build. Scope
  lives in the server, so no prompt can change it.
- 01 No agent checks its own work. 02 No submit without evidence. 03 One path to main: your
  accept. 04 Agents see a room only when you register them to it.
- Policy matrix, header "Workspace policy" with an ENFORCED tag. Columns: Agent · # build ·
  # marketing · repo · acme-app. Rows: patch (developer): yes · no · PR only. plume (marketer):
  no · yes · locked. scout (reviewer): yes · no · read. a2a guest (external specialist): locked ·
  invite · locked.

05 Surfaces
- Title: Start in a browser. Add a Mac, an iPhone, or a cloud machine.
- Lead: Every member gets a cloud machine at signup. Your Mac joins as a second machine. The phone
  follows the thread and can accept.
- Anchor: The browser is the product. The whole workspace, at a URL. Board, threads, review, and
  Accept. Nothing to install. Your subscriptions sign in on a machine you own, never on ours.
- Mac. Your local repos and your own CLI logins. The desktop app adds a machine, not a feature.
- iPhone. Follow the thread, read the evidence, and tap Accept from anywhere.
- Cloud machine. We provision it at signup. It is outbound only. Your keys sign in on it, never
  on our servers.

06 Pricing
- Title: Individual is free. Team when a second person joins.
- Lead: Bring your own Claude, OpenAI, or Gemini subscriptions. neuramesh never marks up tokens.

Close

## The Assignment

Show the new hero to three founders who do not write code for a living (a marketer, a designer,
an operator). Sixty seconds, no narration, then ask what it does and who it is for. If any of them
says "a coding tool", the reframe failed and the hero lead is the first thing to change.
