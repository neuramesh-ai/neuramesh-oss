The four themes, paired

Graphite is the pick for dark. This adds the other half of the question: the light side. Nothing about the dark change touches the light themes — they're separate blocks in tokens.css, so Light and Cream Oak ship exactly as they are today unless you also move them. But Graphite leaves Light without a partner: one family got crisper, the other didn't. Paper is Light run through the same derivation as Graphite — anchor the dominant plane, tighten the steps, let borders carry the edges — mirrored toward white instead of black.

The screen from your screenshot: window backdrop, nav panel, the floating main well, the composer. --panel2 covers roughly half the pixels, which is why it's the token that decides how a theme "reads".

Scene 2 — Thread

Where the dependent tokens live: a floating popover, a code block, status chips, role-tinted avatars, the accept button. Each was tuned against its theme's old ground.

The surface ladders

Both families make the same move in opposite directions: shift the whole ladder ~12 points away from mid, keep the step sizes, and widen the gap between the ground and its border so edges stay crisp on a flatter surface.

Token diff

Shaded rows carry the change. Every FSM colour, role colour and link stays exactly as shipped in both families — only greys move.

What breaks in each direction

Dark · code blocks invert

--code: #131313 was darker than every panel. At Graphite it lands lighter than --panel2 and every code block becomes a glowing slab. Drops to #0a0a0a.

Dark · popovers lose their float

--overlay: var(--panel) works because shadows read on grey. On a near-black ground rgba(0,0,0,.5) is invisible. Fix: --overlay becomes its own lifted surface — elevation from light, not shade.

Light · overlays are already fine

Worth being straight about this: a dark shadow reads perfectly well on light paper, so light has no elevation problem to fix. Paper's --overlay: #ffffff is a one-point crispness nudge, not a repair — the dark-side --overlay change is the load-bearing one.

Light · edges go mushy if borders follow

Brightening the ground without darkening the borders flattens every panel into the backdrop. Paper lifts the surfaces and pulls --border2 down (#d6cfc4 → #cec5b7), so edge contrast goes up, not down.

Both · tint washes drift

--green-soft and --violet-soft were mixed against the old ground. Unchanged, they'd read too bright in dark and too dull in light. Both re-mix with the ladder.

Dark · the launch flash

Electron's backgroundColor: '#121212' is hardcoded in main/index.ts and never tracked the theme. It happens to be right for Graphite — worth pinning deliberately rather than by luck.

If you'd rather not touch light at all

Entirely reasonable — Light is not broken, and shipping Graphite alone is a smaller, lower-risk change. The cost is only that the two families stop rhyming: dark gets a crisp/soft pair, light keeps a warm/warmer one. Flip between Graphite and Current light above to judge whether that bothers you.
