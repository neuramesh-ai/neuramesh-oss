Sessions — one spine for chats, tasks, and rooms

The report: a Home send opens a chat with rex that feels disconnected from the channel and its messages. The mechanics agree with the feeling: the send lands in a real room, but the room's primary surface is a feed that shows only the conversation's root — every reply lives in a sheet floating over it. Close the sheet and the room looks dead while the conversation thrives. Meanwhile history lives in a third place (the Recents rail). Three planes, one conversation.

The research: Claude desktop (Apr 2026 redesign) and the Codex app converged on the same shape — a sidebar of sessions grouped by project; the container carries the crew/config/context; one session = one full surface; sessions self-archive when their work merges; no feed anywhere. NeuraMesh's data model is already session-shaped (threads with a frozen mode, thread-per-task). Only the shell isn't.

The proposal: keep channels — they are the crew, the skills, the board, the ACL, the work-type category — but make them folders of sessions instead of feeds. A session is a chat or a task; the same row appears in Home, in its room, and in Recents because it is the same thing. No schema change. The feed retires; its two real jobs (digest, announcements) survive as a pinned room brief and as ordinary sessions.
