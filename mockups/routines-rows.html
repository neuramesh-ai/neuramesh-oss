Routines as rows — a list of standing behaviours, not a wall of cards

A card is for one thing you look at; a list is for many things you scan. Two routines fill a 1500px screen with two half-empty cards and a hectare of nothing under them — and the thing you actually came to read (what runs, when, and is it still armed) is spread across three zones of each card instead of down three columns you can compare. Rows fix the scan: the cadence lines up, the titles line up, the next-fire lines up, and twenty routines read as fast as two.

The anatomy is the bell popover's, at destination scale — same row rhythm, same hover, same section rule — so the two lists in the product are one idiom rather than two. Hover a row.

Stop 1The list — cadence · title · prompt · when, in four scannable columns
Every routine's differences sit on vertical axes you can compare: the cadence chip at a fixed 96px so WEEKDAYS · 09:00 and FRIS · 15:00 stack, the title at a capped width so the prompts start on the same column, and the countdown right-aligned. The prompt gets one line, not two: on this surface it is the identifying detail, not the content — the full text is one click away in the editor, and a routine you cannot identify from one line has a title problem, not a layout problem. Paused rows stay in the list, dimmed with their cadence chip going grey, rather than in a second visual class — a paused routine is the one you are most likely to be looking for.

Stop 2What the cards were spending, and on what
Same two routines, as they render today. Each card is three stacked zones — head, prompt, footer — separated by a rule, in a two-up grid. Nothing here is badly drawn; it is simply the wrong container: a card says look at this one thing, and a schedule inventory is a thing you scan. The cost lands in three places: the cadence and the countdown sit at the card's right edge, so they never line up between cards; the prompt is given two lines of a paragraph it does not need; and the two verbs sit in permanent view on every card, which is how a list of five becomes ten buttons you are not using.

Stop 3The spec

The row

  • One line, 40px, --r-lg. Four slots on fixed axes: cadence chip (96px min) · title (max 44%) + prompt (flex, one line, ellipsis) + #room · meta (next · runs).
  • The cadence chip is the leading glyph — it says WHEN, the one thing every routine differs by, and it is what you scan the column for. It wears --prog; a paused row's goes --closed.
  • Due soon (under 2h) turns the countdown --warn and bold, unchanged from today — it is the one urgency this surface has.
  • Paused rows stay in the list, dimmed, sorted after the active ones. A paused routine is the one you are most likely hunting for; a second section would hide it.
  • The whole row opens the editor, as the card did. role="button", Enter, and the same title tooltip.

The hover (the bell's, at destination scale)

  • Rest: transparent on the sheet. Hover: lifts onto the card surface — --card + hairline + --shadow-card in light, --panel2 flat in the darks (a shadow on near-black is invisible; the ground step is the signal there).
  • The verbs are icons, not word pills (George, 2026-08-16): two labelled pills per row put ~14 characters of chrome on every line of a list you scan, saying the same two words all the way down. Same 26px measure and wash as the task header's .navpin; the data-tip bubble carries the word on approach and aria-label carries it always. Armed, Remove goes danger with a ring — a two-step confirm carried by colour alone would be no confirm at all, so the tooltip and the label both say what the second click does.
  • The ledger steps aside and the verbs rise in its place — Pause/Resume and Remove occupy the same fixed slot the meta does, so the row never reflows and the list never jitters under the pointer. One object moving, not two fading past each other.
  • The › fades as the verbs arrive: it promises "this opens", and while your pointer is on Remove that promise is wrong.
  • All of it --dur-fast / --ease, and all of it off under prefers-reduced-motion — the verbs then simply show on hover.

What does not change

  • The ScopeBar, the ＋ New automation button, the two-step Remove (sure?), the 5s poll, the editor modal, nextRunLabel, the counts the nav badge reads.
  • The hint line is gone (George): "each run fires its prompt into a room" explained what every row now shows one line below it — cadence, #room — and as a bare child of the view it never lined up with the scope bar's inset. Alignment was the symptom; redundancy was the reason.
  • The empty state keeps its serif line and its button — an empty destination is the one place a page should still speak.
  • Calendar is untouched. It is the time view; this is the inventory. Rows here make the difference between them sharper, not blurrier.
