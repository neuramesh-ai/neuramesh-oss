Workspace switching & invitations

round 1 · design gate for the multi-workspace fix

00What this round decides

Today a person can belong to two workspaces and the desktop shows only one — the oldest, picked by resolved[0]. If the workspace you were invited to happens to be older than your own, the app switches into it on next boot and wipes your local replica on the way. There is no switcher, no way to leave, and an invitation that lands while you are already signed in is never claimed at all.

Three surfaces have to exist before that can be fixed: somewhere to see and switch workspaces, an invitation you accept or decline rather than one that silently joins you, and a way to leave. Everything below is drawn from the app's own tokens and control geometry.

01Where the switcher lives

The nav dock moves — left, top, or right — and in a side dock the project name lives on the spine, not in a header. The account menu is the only surface present in all three positions, and it already holds the identity ladder this belongs to: who you are → what you pay → which workspace you are standing in. The workspace is an account-level fact, so it goes there.

The here pill is the load-bearing detail. Your machine registers to exactly one workspace (machines.workspace_id), so its agents are online in one place at a time. The pill says where they are. It is not decoration — without it, "why is bosun offline in Acme?" has no answer on screen.

With one workspace the section collapses to nothing: no Workspaces heading, no rows, no "New workspace". The menu is byte-for-byte what it is today. A switcher nobody needs should not be visible.

03Switching restarts the app — so it says so

WS is a single global read in 113 places, and the agent host is a one-per-process singleton with no teardown. Switching workspaces re-registers the machine, re-resolves credentials and policies, and rebinds every query — which is a relaunch, the same path sign-out already takes. That is a 2-second cost the user should be told about, not surprised by. The replica is not wiped: both workspaces already stream into it, so the resync is instant.

04An invitation you answer

Signing in stops joining you to anything. A pending invite for your verified address becomes a card on Home, above the needs-you queue, because it needs you and it is not a task. Join writes the membership; Decline closes the invite and frees the address for a re-invite.

Two cards, not one flow: joining and switching are separate acts. Joining is free and instant; switching costs a restart. Fusing them would make every invitation a forced context swap.

05First run with an invitation — the trap

This is the state that breaks if we get it wrong. Once signing in no longer auto-joins, an invited new user arrives with zero memberships — and today zero memberships means needsOnboarding = true, so the wizard cheerfully walks them into creating their own second workspace instead of joining the one they were invited to. Boot has to check for a pending invite before it decides onboarding.

06Members: invite, decline, remove, leave

The Members tab grows the states the new commands make real. A declined invite is not an error — it frees the address, so re-inviting is a normal act. And Leave workspace finally exists, which is what makes the two long-standing dead-end messages true: workspace deletion and account deletion both tell you to leave first, and until now there was no way to.

07What is enforced, and where

Per the doctrine: invariants live in the schema and the server, never in the UI's good manners. The screens above are the only way to reach these, but none of them are what makes them true.

RuleEnforced inHow
An invite grants nothing until acceptedserver Sign-in stops writing workspace_members. Membership only from workspace.accept_invite.
You can only accept your own inviteserver Accept re-reads the caller's verified email from Clerk. The invite id alone is never sufficient.
One live invite per address per workspaceschema The partial unique index on (workspace_id, lower(email)) where status='pending' — already there. A decline frees the slot by leaving that predicate.
A free workspace never exceeds 3 seatsserver Counted at invite and re-counted at accept — an invite can sit for 14 days while the room fills.
The owner can't leaveserver workspace.leave rejects the owner; transfer or delete instead.
Switching never destroys local dataclient shouldWipeReplica takes the membership set and refuses to wipe a workspace you still belong to. Pure function, unit-tested.
Boot never moves you silentlyclient pickBootWorkspace prefers your last workspace while you're still a member; [0] is the fallback, not the rule.
One workspace's rows never appear in anotherclient Every replica query filters on workspace. Audited by a script that must catch a known-bad query before it is trusted.

One thing this round deliberately does not fix: a machine still belongs to one workspace at a time, so your agents are only online where you're standing. Multi-workspace agent hosting is a separate, much larger change — the here pill and the switch sheet exist to make the current limit legible rather than to hide it.
