The orchestrator owns the flow

Today rex is a router: it creates a task, hands it to a seated agent, and steps out — the board FSM and the daemon watches carry it from there. The proposal is that rex becomes the general contractor: it holds the task for its whole life, spawns subagents per phase from the seated agents as seat templates, reviews what comes back, and faces the human at every gate.

Three things below are worth confirming before any code: the shape of each flow, the four constraints that decide what is actually buildable, and which parts of today's model survive.

What changes

today

rex routes, then leaves

human asks→ rex triages→ offer_task→ market-analyst claims→ submits→ reviewer→ accept

Rex's own turn ends at offer_task. Everything after is the FSM plus watches. The observed symptom: the worker claims and starts before rex's digest posts, so the thread reads backwards — the ordering bug is this architecture leaking into the transcript, not a sort bug.

proposed

rex owns, and answers for it

human asks→ rex owns #N→ spawns legs→ rex reviews→ gate↻ rex continues→ seated reviewer→ accept

Rex is assignee_id for the task's whole life. Each phase is subagents it spawns and owns. The ↻ is the load-bearing detail: a human gate ends rex's turn and re-wakes it later — which is why the brain shipped in v0.75.0 is the precondition for any of this.

The four constraints

Two are hard limits that the design must bend around. Two are consequences to accept deliberately.

hard · server-enforced
Rex cannot review its own work
SELF_REVIEW_BLOCKED fires when the approver is the assignee, and a subagent has no board identity at all — so it cannot approve either. Review therefore stays a seated reviewer that is not rex. This is doctrine §4 working as intended, and the plan must not "solve" it.
hard · physics
A subagent lives inside one turn
sliceBudget carves a child's budget from the parent's remainder, so a leg cannot outlive the turn that spawned it. A build spans days and several human gates. Ownership is durable; execution is per-turn. Rex is re-woken at each gate and reads its subject brain to continue.
consequence
Budgets have to grow
A triage turn is 4 min / 24k. Spawning three designers from inside it is not affordable — each child takes half the remainder. An owning turn needs its own kind and budget, or the first fan-out starves the second.
consequence
Every board card says "rex"
The assignee stops being the answer to who is doing this. The phase spectrum and the subagent tree carry it instead — which is why the leg rows became clickable first. Worth confirming you want the board to read this way.

Today a running leg gets a 6px dot fading between 100% and 45% opacity over 1.4s — slow enough to read as idle. Four candidates, all monochrome so the card's "shape carries state" rule holds.

What survives from today

StaysWhy
Seated role agents (designer, architect, developer, marketer)They become the seat templates a subagent inherits — model, tools, brief, skills. Rex resolves a seat by specialism first: if someone in the room is specialised in what the leg needs, the leg runs on their config and the card says @iris · opus. If nothing matches, it falls back to a plain role default and says so — a hollow glyph rather than a borrowed name, because implying a specialist that does not exist is the worse failure. They also remain offerable for work that wants a durable owner.
The seated reviewerHard constraint above. An independent check is the point, and it is the one role that must not be rex's own subagent.
The board FSM and its gatesUnchanged. approve_design, approve_ship_plan and accept stay HUMAN_ONLY; rex owning the row does not soften any of them.
SubtasksStill the way companion work rides a parent. A spawned leg is not a subtask — it is execution, not a board entity.
Delegation, as an optionLong repo-backed builds with a worktree and a PR still suit a durable seated owner. Rex should be able to choose it — the change is that owning becomes the default, not that delegation is deleted.

Three defects this also settles. The ordering bug dissolves — with no hand-off there is no claim message racing rex's digest (and for any delegated path, rex's digest posts before the offer command rather than after). The boring dot is the indicator round above. The stale spinner after submit is separate and real: beats declared but never advanced are left running when the task moves to in_review, so step 5 of 7 spins forever. That wants settling at the phase transition, not at the end of a turn that may already have died.
