Design round · left rail · 2026-07-28

Give the rail a way to get out of the way

The left rail is 266px of the window, always — a fifth of a 1280 screen, held by a project header, four rooms, one person, seven agents and an account row. On a task thread or a board that width is the difference between a comfortable read and a cramped one. Three directions below, all reversible, all machine-local. Each frame is real: same tokens, same proportions, both themes.

My recommendation: A, with C's peek as a follow-on

A (Vitals rail) is the only direction that keeps the rail earning its width when it has none. This app's left rail is not a menu — it is a presence board. Names are the least valuable thing on it; the unread dot on #dev and the green ping on patch are the reason you glance left at all. A rail that keeps exactly those and drops the labels loses almost nothing you were actually reading, and 210px is a real gain.

B is the cheapest to build and the most defensible on doctrine — it is literally the marketing brand rail's collapse (.mkrailtab), so it adds zero new vocabulary. But it makes you blind: an agent finishing, a room going unread, all of it is gone until you re-open, and re-opening to check is worse than never having collapsed.

C is not really a third direction — it is a modifier that sits on top of either. It is also the one most likely to annoy: a panel that flies out when the pointer drifts left is the classic "motion where calm was wanted." If we do it, it should be keyboard-and-click only (⌘\, or clicking the rail), never a bare hover trigger.

So: ship A, controlled by a chevron that lives in the frame top beside the wordmark (where the crumb starts — the one place that is chrome in every view) plus ⌘\. Persist to localStorage like nm:mkrail-collapsed and the theme already do — a machine-local preference, never synced. Then, if the compact rail still feels lossy in use, add C's click-to-peek on top of it.

What I need from you

  • Direction — A, B, or C (or A now, C later, which is my vote).
  • The collapsed floor — in A, do agents stay visible when there are 15 of them? My proposal: show the busy ones first, capped at 8, then a +7 count that opens the full roster overlay we already have.
  • Auto-collapse? — should opening a task panel collapse the rail on its own and restore it on close? Powerful, but it is the app moving furniture while you work. My vote: no, keep it manual.
  • Scope — this change rides the task-panel PR, or lands as its own?
