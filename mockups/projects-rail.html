The projects rail — the fold idiom, turned into the work axis

George's shape (2026-08-01, round 2): “a slice of B but better designed… right now we have a nice transparent vertical bar when the left nav is collapsed; can we do something similar on the leftmost rail but for the projects; vertical text shows with the active project name. On hover of the rail it reveals the project icons showing active and others, and clicking one switches the channels, recents/history. Home can be there as well, styled separately.”

Why this is better than my B. B added a 52px column that was always open and always paying for itself in width. This one costs 30px and borrows a mechanic the app already ships: .navfoldtab — transparent strip, writing-mode: vertical-rl mono, hover lifts it (tokens.css:758–770). It is not a new structural element; it is the fold idiom pointed at a different noun.

And it quietly settles round 1. Direction A's argument was that the rail's vertical order should be the scope contract — workspace above the rule, project below it. This rail is that same contract rotated into its own column: Home and the account sit in the strip (workspace), the whole 266px nav beside it is the project. A and B were not alternatives; this is their intersection, and it is smaller than either.

At rest30px, transparent, and it says where you are
The strip carries four things and nothing else: Home with its needs-you count (workspace scope, styled as a filled tile — the only filled object in the strip), a hairline rule, the active project as a ringed tile with its name in vertical mono, and one quiet dot per other project — which is both the "there is more here" affordance and where a live agent elsewhere shows up. The account tile closes it. Everything to the right of the rule belongs to the named project; nothing to the left of it does.

The mechanics that make hover safe paper

open
140ms intent — crossing the strip on the way somewhere else opens nothing
close
240ms grace — a diagonal move to a project row does not dismiss it under the cursor
width
150ms ease — inside the ≤150ms motion budget (CLAUDE.md #2)
layout
0px shift — the reveal is position:absolute over the nav; the app never reflows
15Hover is a shortcut, never the only door. Click the strip pins it open (persisted per machine, like nm:navFolded); ⌘⇧P opens it from the keyboard; ↑↓ + ⏎ pick; Esc closes. ⌘K keeps its Switch to … commands unchanged.
16The one honest risk. This strip sits where macOS puts the traffic lights and where a full-screen window puts the menu-bar hot corner. The intent delay is what keeps it from firing on a mis-aimed mouse — and it is the thing to check first on the preview harness, not in a mockup.
17prefers-reduced-motion: no width animation, no pulse on the dots — the reveal snaps and the live dot becomes a static ring.
RecommendationThe rail at 30px · R2's named reveal · the fold absorbed
Build it. The idea is stronger than either round-1 direction it descends from, and it is cheaper: it reuses the fold's own vocabulary (transparent strip, vertical mono, hover lift), it deletes the false promise at the nav head rather than annotating it, and it makes the project visible at every rail width including folded — which today's nav cannot do at all.

R2 over R1 for one concrete reason: two of this workspace's three projects would render as bare letters, and default collides with the first dev-* project anyone makes. R1's 52px saves 164px of overlay that costs nothing (it floats over the nav) and buys nothing you can act on. Keep R1's tile size — 34px — as R2's row glyph budget so the two stay one component if we ever want the dense mode.
