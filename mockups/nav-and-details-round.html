Three cuts — the orb in the rail, the nav that stops nesting, the details that leave the thread

Design round for George's three reports (2026-08-17), each one a place where a rule the app already settled was applied on some of the surfaces it was written for. ① A rail row with an agent inside it draws a 5 px pulsing dot — the pre-orb spinner — while every other live surface wears a thinking orb. ② The nav nests one collapsible header, one channel strip and one n more › per project, so four projects spend eight rows of chrome before the first thread. ③ A content thread still renders the brand panel inside the sheet, three inches from the Workbench that was built in the same round to hold exactly that.

Everything below is drawn from the real tokens (tokens.css, verbatim above) and the real markup classes, in both shipping themes. Recommendations are marked. Deviations from an etched ruling are marked and named — they need your call, not my judgement.

Stop ①The live rail row wears the orb

The orb already exists and already has a state map: ui/Orb.tsx wraps thinking-orbs, and orbStateFor(verb, cat, role) — the table settled in the agent-stream-status round — is what RunCard, ThreadLiveBar, AgentGhost, BellPopover and the brand rail all read. The nav rail is the one live surface still drawing the thing the orb replaced: a 5px --prog dot on the verb line (.nhlind) plus a pulsing halo on the state dot. Two pulses, and neither says what kind of work is happening.

Two ways to land it. A puts the orb in the row's glyph gutter — the liveness column becomes one object instead of two; B swaps only the verb-line indicator and leaves the gutter alone. The orbs below are the real animation (a vanilla port of the package, as in mockups/agent-stream-status), not a drawing.

Stop ②The nav stops nesting — one scope row, flat rows

Today every project costs a header row, a channel strip, and an n more › to show three threads — and every thread row spends its first line on #room, repeated straight down the column. Four projects = eight rows of chrome and three separate cap decisions before the rail has said anything you came for.

The move is not a new mechanism — it is the app's own ScopeBar, which every workspace destination (Tasks · Whiteboards · Automations · Files · Skills · Activity) already wears since 2026-08-07: narrowing is a visible, reversible choice, never an invisible default. The nav is the last surface answering "what am I looking at?" its own way. The PROJECTS eyebrow becomes the scope row; the rows go flat and recency-ordered; and the row names what the scope doesn't — the same rule SessionRow's showRoom already implements on Home.

Stop ③The details panel leaves the thread

The 2026-08-16 round already made this call — “two panels at the window's right edge is exactly the duplication this round exists to remove” — and landed it on one of the two thread kinds. TaskThread portals its sections into the Workbench when a slot exists. ConvoThread never portals: it mounts <ThreadRail> inline, unconditionally. So a content conversation in a marketing room draws Brand docs · Upcoming · Connections inside the sheet — the exact panel the Workbench was built to hold. It is not a second decision; it is the first one, unfinished.

The rule to finish it with is the one the Workbench already follows for Code and Artifacts: the face is scoped to whatever you are standing in. A task → its description, requirements, Definition of Done, artifacts, subtasks. A chat thread → its artifacts, plus the room's sections. A room home with nothing open → the room's sections alone. One panel, one edge, one door (⌘P and the thread head's dock-right pin).
