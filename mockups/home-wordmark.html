Home watermark — the quiet-state signature

OpenCode's wordmark works because their home is empty: an enormous near-invisible mark fills the dead space and points the eye at the one control. Ours is not empty — Home carries needs-you and in-flight, and docs/33 §1 is explicit that chrome stays out of the content's way. So the treatment is built as a quiet-state signature: it lives in the band directly above the composer, sinks behind it (clipped, never floating), bleeds past the reading column — and pulls back to a third of its ink the moment the queue has something in it. The rules this obeys

Clipped, not floating

The mark's bottom is cut by the composer's top edge. Floating it above reads as a stray logo; sinking it behind reads as a surface.

Cropped horizontally

It bleeds past the 720px reading column and the sheet clips it. Cropping is what turns a logo into texture.

Ink is derived

opacity = base × (1 − min(1, items/4)), floored at ~a third. An empty queue is the only time it's at full strength — the emptier your day, the more the brand shows.

Dark stays achromatic

Cream may carry the oak; graphite and soft-dark use plain white ink at low alpha. No warmth migrates into dark (founder ruling, v0.47.1).

Never a second display moment

Home already speaks in serif at the greeting. Whatever lands here must not read as a competing headline — which is the whole case for keeping it under ~6% ink.
