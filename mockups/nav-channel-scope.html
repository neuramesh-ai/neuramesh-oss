The channel stops being a place you go

The ask (George, 2026-08-03): left nav shows Automations · New chat · Whiteboard. Channels become a searchable text-style dropdown filter at the top, defaulting to All, which scopes Recents, Home and Automations. Rex triages the first prompt and files it into a channel automatically — #general when it genuinely can't decide. Routines leave the individual channel homes.

Verdict: three of four are right, and one of them is the best idea in the set. Auto-filing removes the single decision a human should never have had to make, and it closes a defect this repo has already written down — docs/35 §1: "a send that lands in #general looks exactly like a send that lands in #dev." Today you must guess the room correctly and you can't see the consequence. Both halves die here.

Where it lives (round 2, George): the selector stays in the left nav — its head row, caret on the right — not in the main surface. That is the stronger placement, and there's an argument on the record for it: App.tsx:16720 retired the old nav head with "a name at the top of a column promises the column." A channel name at the top of that column keeps the promise — Recents, the destinations and the surface under it are all scoped to exactly that. And the hierarchy reads down the left edge in one line: spine = project · nav head = channel · rail = sessions.

The one correction. docs/35 §8 already considered and rejected "channels become pure labels/filters" — "the crew, board, brief and ACL need a face. A label cannot hold a kanban or a roster." That objection still stands, and the fix is one line: the dropdown is not a filter over chat rows, it is the scope knob for everything below and beside it. All = the workspace lens. #dev = #dev's home — brief, crew, board, settings — rendered in the main surface exactly as it is today. Rooms keep their face; they stop being a list you scroll past to reach anything else.

Whiteboard is the one I'd hold. It has no job yet — see Stop 6. Everything else here is buildable in three slices with no schema change.

Stop 6Whiteboard — the one I'd hold, and what I'd ask first
This isn't a no. It's that the litmus can't be run on it yet: does it make plan → fan out → execute → validate → review → accept faster, safer, or more delightful? A whiteboard could serve at least three different jobs, and they have three different (much cheaper) answers. Name the job and the build gets obvious.
already has a home

“Somewhere to see the designs”

Mockups already render inline in the thread as self-contained HTML; the approved round becomes the visual contract in .nm-evidence/design/ and auto-promotes into the channel artifact library.

A whiteboard here would be a fourth home for one artifact — and the docs/32 §1 lesson is that two surfaces disagreeing about one noun is the bug, not the feature.

exists, unfinished

“Somewhere to see the work spatially”

That's the board plus the phase spectrum (docs/24) — derived, never stored, already drawn on every card as a 4px whisper.

If the complaint is that the board doesn't show relationships (this blocks that, these three are one epic), the honest fix is dependencies on tasks — a schema change with a clear payoff — not a free-draw canvas nothing can read.

the one that might be real

“Somewhere to think with an agent before there's a task”

A shared canvas where you sketch boxes and iris reads them. This is genuinely new — and it is the only version where the agent is a participant rather than a viewer.

But it needs an agent contract first: what can an agent read off a canvas, what can it write back, how does that become a task. Without that it's a drawing app inside a collaboration platform. Ask: is this the job? If yes it deserves its own design round, not a nav slot.

What I'd put in the slot instead, if the nav wants a fourth destination: Library (the artifact library is workspace-wide, curated, and today lives behind a burger menu) — or nothing. An empty slot costs less than a surface with no job.
Stop 7Alternatives, and the four things that must not break
directionshapeverdict
A · pure filter channels become labels; the dropdown filters lists only; rooms cease to be surfaces Rejected — this is docs/35 §8 direction A wearing a new hat. The board, brief, roster, marketing Calendar/Library and channel settings have nowhere to live.
B · scope knob in the nav head one row at the top of the left nav scopes everything under and beside it: All = workspace lens, #room = that room's home. Rooms keep their face; the rail stops listing them Recommended — gets the ask (one selector, defaults to All, no room-picking before you type) while keeping every face, and leaves the main surface exactly as it is.
C · keep the rail, add auto-filing ship only the triage half; the channel list stays exactly as it is Viable fallback — auto-filing is independently valuable and is slice 1 either way. If the knob proves wrong in dogfooding, this is where you stop.
D · nullable channel (an Inbox) threads.channel_id becomes nullable; unfiled sessions live in a real Inbox until rex files them Rejected — conceptually cleanest, but it touches the sync rules, the ACL, and every query that assumes a channel, to buy ~4 seconds of honesty that a "filing…" tag buys for free.
Honest limits — what the build must not strand:
invariant

A session must never change project

Channels belong to exactly one project and exactly one project is active. Triage picks only from the active project's channels, or the session vanishes from the view that created it.

invariant

Filing freezes when work starts

Once a task exists, its channel binds the board, the roster and the branch. An agent may file a thread once, before its first task. A human may move any chat thread, any time.

invariant

The turn must not double

rex already wakes on every human channel message. Filing rides that turn — no pre-pass classifier — or agent overhead breaks the <10% budget for a label.

watch it

The #general black hole

If "can't decide" is cheap, everything lands in #general and the channel axis quietly dies. Store the decision + the reason on the move. If more than ~half of filings land in #general, the rooms are wrong — not the triage.

watch it

The room you can't see any more

The rail list was also the answer to "what rooms exist?". The knob must show every room on open, with unread — and an unread room while the knob is closed needs a dot on the trigger, or a quiet room becomes an invisible one.

watch it

Filing is a visibility change

A channel is the ACL boundary for agents. Filing into #dev grants #dev's roster sight of the conversation. Harmless today (sync is workspace-wide, one human) — it stops being harmless the day channels become private.
