Shared compute — consent, visibility, attribution

round 2 · a machine is lent, not taken

00The rule — a grant, and the ladder that reads it

Today (shipped in 0118) any member can point their requests at anyone’s machine, and failover can recruit any capable machine — both without asking the person whose subscription pays. This round makes lending explicit: the machine’s owner grants a member access, and every rung below rung 1 can only choose among machines granted to the person the work came from.

  1. Capability — a machine that can’t serve the runtime never claims. Unchanged.
  2. The conversation’s machine — an existing thread keeps the machine holding its files. Deliberately NOT gated on the grant: the work already ran there and the files already exist. Follow-ups keep working even after a grant is revoked (§07 says so out loud, and offers the stop).
  3. Your per-agent choice — only machines granted to you are offerable.
  4. Your default machine — same gate.
  5. Origin affinity — your own machine. Never needs a grant; it is yours.
  6. Failover — after the grace window, among granted machines only. An ungranted machine stays out even when it is the only one awake.

Migration matters here. Every existing workspace has been sharing implicitly since 0114 — so the migration backfills a grant between every current member pair. Consent applies to what happens next; nobody’s working setup breaks on deploy.

08Revoking — say what actually stops, and offer the rest

Turning the switch off stops new conversations immediately. Threads already living on your machine keep running there, because rung 1 is continuity and their files are on your disk. That is a real loophole if it goes unsaid — so the confirm says it, counts it, and gives you the second lever.
